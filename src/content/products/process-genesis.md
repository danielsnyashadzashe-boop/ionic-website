## Why one platform instead of six

Most transformation programmes fail on integration, not capability. An
organisation buys process mining from one vendor, document extraction from
another, a workflow engine from a third, then spends the budget that was meant
for change management on making them talk to each other.

Process Genesis was built the other way round. Discovery feeds the workflow
engine directly. The decision engine reads from the same event stream the
analytics layer reports on. Document extraction writes into processes rather
than into an export folder. There is no integration layer between the
components because there are no seams to integrate.

That architecture is where the 60–70% reduction in total cost of ownership
comes from — not from being cheaper per seat, but from removing five
procurement cycles, five security reviews and five sets of integration work.

## What deployment actually looks like

Engagements begin with discovery rather than software. We map how work moves
through the organisation as it is, not as the process documentation claims, and
quantify what automation is worth before anything is configured.

That order matters. It is why the Old Mutual engagement began as a pilot and
became a five-year agreement across all business units, and why the Bidvest
deployment started inside two divisions with group-wide expansion contingent on
the result.
