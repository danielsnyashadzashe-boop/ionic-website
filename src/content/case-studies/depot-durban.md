## The engagement

A depot and warehousing operator in Durban had a new client ready to come on
board. The client had system integration requirements, which is normal in
logistics, where the depot has to speak to the customer's systems before a
single pallet moves.

The quickest timeline available was six months.

That is the entire problem, and it is a commercial one rather than a technical
one. A six-month integration timeline does not mean the work takes six months.
It means the revenue starts in six months, the client waits six months to find
out whether the relationship works, and somewhere in those six months there is
a decent chance they take the business elsewhere.

## What was built

A full ERP, in a month, covering operations, logistics, finance and reporting,
with the integrations the incoming client required.

Two things made that timeline possible, and neither of them is heroics.

**The scope was the business, not a feature list.** Mapping the depot's
operation first meant the build was aimed at how the place actually runs
rather than at a specification assembled from meetings.

**The integration was treated as the deadline, not as a later phase.** The
reason the alternative quote was six months is that integration is usually
scheduled after the core system is standing up. Here the client's integration
requirements set the shape of the build from the first week, because they were
the thing with a date attached.

## The outcome

The new client was onboarded in **ten days**.

The platform came in at **more than 50% below the closest competing quote**.

The depot now runs operations, logistics, finance and reporting on one system
it owns, rather than on the system it would have been given in six months'
time.

## The honest reading of these numbers

Ten days is the onboarding, not the build. The platform took about a month.
The ten-day figure is what the depot could tell its new client once the
platform existed, and it is the number that mattered commercially, so it is
the one that leads. It is not a claim that an ERP can be delivered in ten
days.

The cost comparison is against the quotes this client actually received for
this scope. It is a real comparison on a real deal rather than an industry
average, which makes it more useful and also means it is one data point.
