## The engagement

Old Mutual came to Ionic with the problem that defines large financial services
groups: not a shortage of process documentation, but a shortage of confidence
that the documentation described reality. Multiple business units, each with its
own operating history, each convinced its processes were the exception.

The work began as a pilot rather than a programme. That was deliberate on both
sides — a discovery exercise against one bounded set of processes, with the
explicit understanding that expansion would depend on the result.

## What changed

Discovery came before configuration. Process Genesis mapped how work actually
moved through the pilot area, quantified where time was being lost, and put a
number against what automation was worth before any workflow was built.

The pilot succeeded on the strength of that sequencing, and converted into a
five-year engagement covering Process Genesis deployment and digital
transformation services across all business units.

## Why it scaled

The characteristic Old Mutual singled out was not the automation itself but the
ability to understand complex business units — which is the harder half of the
problem. Automating a process you have misunderstood produces a faster wrong
answer.

Complexity across units is exactly the condition in which a single integrated
platform earns its keep. Every additional business unit brought into a
multi-vendor stack multiplies integration work. Bringing one into Process
Genesis does not.
