## The engagement

The Kruse Group operates two businesses in southern Alberta: Kruse Glass and
Aluminum, and Prestige Windows and Doors. Between them they cover commercial
and residential glazing, which means estimating, fabrication, scheduling,
installation crews, vehicles, materials and safety compliance, all of which
have to agree with each other on any given morning.

Three problems were tangled together, and they are worth naming separately
because they usually are.

**A vendor that was hard to leave.** The commercial operation ran on a system
the group could not easily move off. The cost of that is rarely the licence
fee. It is the slow narrowing of what the business is allowed to do, until the
process is shaped around the software instead of the other way round.

**Manual process in the gaps.** Where the system did not reach, the work
carried on anyway, in spreadsheets, on paper and over the phone.

**Knowledge held by individuals.** A handful of long-serving people knew how
things actually worked. That is a strength right up until one of them is on
leave, and a serious exposure the day one of them retires.

## Mapping before building

We mapped the entire business before designing anything.

This is the part that is tempting to shorten and should not be. A group that
has been running for years on vendor software plus workarounds does not have
one process; it has a documented process and a real one, and the real one is
the one that pays the bills. Automating the documented version produces a
system nobody uses and a business that quietly keeps running on the
workarounds.

So the first deliverable was not software. It was an accurate picture of how
the group actually operates, built with the people who operate it.

## What was built

One commercial operations platform, covering:

- **Front end of the business.** CRM, tenders, estimating and take-offs
- **Delivery.** Scheduling, jobs, fabrication, logistics
- **Assets and people.** Fleet, assets, staff and HR
- **Compliance and control.** Health and safety, quality
- **The numbers.** Finance and reporting

Sixteen functions in one place, where previously they were split across a
vendor system, spreadsheets, paper and individual memory.

**A mobile app puts the field on the same platform.** Glazing work does not
happen at a desk. Crews on site record what they need to record from a phone,
so the office is not waiting on a paper docket to find out what happened
yesterday.

## The outcome

Vendor lock-in is gone. The manual processes that filled the gaps have been
replaced rather than merely digitised. And the knowledge that used to live
with a few individuals is now built into the system, which means the group
keeps it regardless of who is in the building.

The residential side of the group is next.

## Why this one is here

It is the clearest example of the technology-agnostic argument in practice.
The right answer for Kruse was not a product off a shelf, because no shelf
product covers sixteen functions in that particular combination for a glazing
group of that size. The right answer was to understand the business properly
and then build to it.

That is also the honest caveat. This was a custom build, and a custom build is
the correct recommendation only when the alternative genuinely does not fit.
It was the right call here because the mapping came first and showed it.
